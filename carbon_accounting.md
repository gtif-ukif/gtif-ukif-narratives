### A look into point source methane emissions from earth observation data <!--{ style="font-size:1rem;opacity:0.8;margin-top:1rem;" }-->

## Overview

"Researchers conclude that five times more methane is being leaked from oil and gas production than reported."

[cpree.princeton.edu — UK substantially underestimates its methane emissions from oil and gas production](https://cpree.princeton.edu/news/2023/uk-substantially-underestimates-its-methane-emissions-oil-and-gas-production)

There is a growing urgency to reduce fugitive methane emissions to reach climate targets that are being defined by governments initiatives such as the Global Methane Pledge or Europe's Green Deal. In addition to new government regulations coming into place, there have been voluntary targets defined and adopted by some oil and gas companies, e.g., OGMP-2 to help them incrementally reduce their GHG emissions and impact to the environment.

With these new regulations coming into place there is a commercial need to have a solution that can support both regulatory and volunteer compliance.

Below are two examples of significant methane emissions from oil and gas facilities, detected using the Sentinel-2 satellite and a methane emissions retrieval algorithm. This algorithm leverages the short-wave infrared (SWIR) spectral channels to identify emissions and calculate emission rates. The rates are derived by analyzing the plume structure, measured methane concentration, and local wind speed. Future analyses will focus on regions in the UK and Europe to identify major emission sources and examine how these emissions fluctuate over time.
